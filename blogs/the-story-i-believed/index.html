The Story I Believed

Over Diwali my dad asked why I'd stopped reading and doing astrology. My answer came out smooth because I'd rehearsed it, and when I checked the dates afterwards it turned out to be only half of what happened.
November 7, 2026 · 4 min · 715 words · Nachiket Namjoshi

The Question Over Diwali#

My parents came over for Diwali. At some point my dad asked why I don’t do my old hobbies anymore. Reading, astrology. Was I slacking? Being lazy?

I can see why he asked. These days I spend more time with family. I relax with my wife, do wife-things with her: pedicures, massages. I’ve got into gardening. From where he was sitting, his son used to read books or spend hours on the computer. Now he gets pedicures.

The Answer I Gave#

I explained it the way I usually do. Hobbies and interests change. I still enjoy reading. But right now, I love spending time with my wife, family and plants much more. I also told him about the idea I’ve been running on for a while: create more than consume.

It went fine. It was a calm conversation, not an argument, like any other “discussion” with family. I gave my answer pretty fluently, because I’ve given it before to myself, several times.

Checking the Dates#

Later I went back and checked what happened when.

Reading didn’t stop, it reduced. I still read a chapter or two once in a while. But it’s been at least three months since I held an actual book. I haven’t read much online either, especially since I built something that reads and summarises articles for me.

The feeling behind “create more than consume” is older than the phrase. In May 2024 I wrote Content That Has a Job because I was fed up with consuming. That post was about choosing what deserves my attention and cutting down. The “create” half came later, somewhere before I built the summarising thing in April 2026. This blog, in the form you’re reading now, came in September.

And reading didn’t shrink because of the idea. It shrank because of everything else: the wedding, family, work. I’ve had too much on my plate for most of my adult life, as I’ve written about before.

So my dad has his story: maybe I’m slacking. I have mine: my interests changed and I started spending my time differently. And the dates have a third one: life got full, reading got squeezed, and an idea that had been forming since 2024 gave a name to what I built in the gap.

The One I’d Rehearsed#

My answer came out easily. I didn’t have to think when my dad asked. I’d already told it to myself several times, so by the time I told him, it sounded finished.

Deb Liu writes that once you build a story on top of what happened, the story starts deciding what you do, and “gradually over time, the story becomes the truth.” Mine hadn’t gone that far. But I’d been repeating it without once asking what happened first. When I did check, a few dates were enough to show it was only half the picture, and that even the idea in it had grown over time rather than arriving whole.

That doesn’t make it made up. Psychologists who study life stories see them as how people connect events and make sense of how they got here. Mine had something real under it: I was fed up with consuming in 2024, long before this blog.

Where I’ve Landed#

The idea and the lack of time both pushed me into building this. The feeling sat there for a long time without me building anything. Less time on its own could just as easily have meant more scrolling.

What I told my dad left out the part where life got full and reading slowly slipped out of my routine.

There’s one part of my story I haven’t checked at all. “Create more than consume” sounds like I consume less. But something now reads articles for me and turns them into notes I go through every week. Maybe I didn’t stop consuming. Maybe I handed it to something else and stopped counting it. That’s the next thing I want to find out.

As for my dad’s version, it’s not how I’d describe it, and the conversation went fine. But he saw something real, that I’m reading much less, and a story started forming around it. Same as mine did.

I wonder how many other answers I give myself come from the surrounding stories?
